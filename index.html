What this does and doesn't capture

Savings come from less hands-on labor time and fewer reruns, minus the running costs (service and extra consumables). Payback is the upfront cost divided by monthly net savings.

It doesn't capture things that are often the real reason to automate: traceability, audit readiness, consistency between operators, and being able to grow volume without hiring. If payback looks borderline, those usually decide it. It also ignores validation downtime and the learning curve in the first months.

The presets use illustrative numbers, not data from any company.
